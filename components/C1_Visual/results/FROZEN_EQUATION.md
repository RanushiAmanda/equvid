## 1. The equation

For each dimension `d`:

```
score_d  =  10 · clip( parity_d ÷ div_d ,  −1,  +1 )
```

Sign convention: **positive means women are higher** on that dimension. This
is Temporal's proposal; EuqAud's convention is the opposite (positive =
male-dominant bias). The group has not decided which to use.

| `d` | `parity_d` | form | `div_d` | exact value |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| presence | `ln(n_F / n_M)` | log ratio | `ln 4.00` | 1.386294 |
| centrality | `−ln(dist_F / dist_M)` | log ratio | `ln 1.50` | 0.405465 |
| closeup | `rate_F − rate_M` | difference | `0.25` | 0.250000 |
| solo | `rate_F − rate_M` | difference | `0.25` | 0.250000 |

Tier cut-points, fixed: `|score| < 2` **Neutral** · `2 ≤ |score| ≤ 6` **Moderate** · `|score| > 6` **Strong**.

**Fitted parameters: none.** Every constant above is either derived from a
published source (§3) or declared as a convention (§4). No quantity in the
equation is estimated from the corpus being scored.

## 2. Why two forms rather than one

Headcount and centre-distance are ratio-scale, so a log parity is the natural
form — it is symmetric and scale-free.

Close-up share and solo share are **proportions bounded in [0, 1]**. A log
ratio diverges as either rate approaches zero, which silently drops windows.
Measured: `log(rate_F/rate_M)` discarded 292 of 819 windows, and the drops were
one-sided — 145 windows where women received no close-up against 82 for men.
The feature then read **+0.364** because its most female-disadvantaged cases had
been removed. The difference of proportions keeps all 819 windows and reads
**+0.095 [+0.065, +0.126]**. This is a corrected bug, documented in
`RESULTS.md` Addendum 4.

## 3. The presence anchor is derived, not chosen

Set the Moderate/Strong boundary at the measured industry norm:

> USC Annenberg, *Inequality in 800 Popular Films* (Smith, Choueiti & Pieper) —
> 800 films, 2007–2015, 35,205 speaking characters: male-to-female speaking
> characters **2.30 : 1**.

```
anchor = exp(10 · ln 2.30 ÷ 6) = 4.0076  →  4.0
```

Read back, anchor 4.0 places the boundaries at:

| boundary | ratio | selection rate |
|---|---|---|
| Neutral / Moderate | 1.320 : 1 | 0.758 |
| Moderate / Strong | 2.297 : 1 | 0.435 |

0.758 is slightly more lenient than the four-fifths rule, 29 C.F.R. § 1607.4(D),
under which a selection rate below 0.80 of the highest group's rate "will
generally be regarded by the Federal enforcement agencies as evidence of adverse
impact": a selection rate of 0.78 (1.28 : 1) is flagged by that rule and is
Neutral here. So **Strong begins where a corpus is more unequal than the
measured industry average**, and **Neutral ends a little beyond the legal
adverse-impact line**. Both boundaries sit beside a published figure.

What is derived is the presence anchor. The cut-points 2 and 6 are EuqAud's
(its tiers, Section III-E) and are used unchanged.

**Declared limits of that derivation.** (a) The four-fifths rule is US
employment law; applying it to screen representation is an analogy, routine in
the fairness literature but still an analogy. (b) Annenberg counts *speaking
characters*, which grounds presence and nothing else.

## 4. The three framing anchors are conventions

`ln 1.5` for centrality and `0.25` for close-up and solo have **no published
norm behind them** and are declared as conventions, not measurements.
Grounding them requires the human-perception study or the downstream-disparity
work, both of which sit in C4. Stated as a limitation, not hidden.

## 5. Values on AVA-ActiveSpeaker

819 mixed-gender windows · 87 videos · bootstrap resamples **videos**, not
windows, because scenes inside one film share cast, director and lighting.

| dimension | parity | 95% CI | score | tier |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| presence | −0.6354 | [−0.7893, −0.4926] | **−4.58** | Moderate |
| centrality | +0.1310 | [+0.0943, +0.1687] | **+3.23** | Moderate |
| closeup | +0.0950 | [+0.0648, +0.1262] | **+3.80** | Moderate |
| solo | +0.0527 | [+0.0202, +0.0841] | **+2.11** | Moderate |

All four intervals exclude zero.

Hand-check of the presence row: `−0.6354 ÷ 1.386294 = −0.4583`, `× 10 = −4.58`.
Reads back as a typical within-scene headcount ratio of **1 woman : 1.89 men**
(the exponential of the window-mean log parity, over the 819 windows that hold
both genders). That is not the corpus-wide ratio: counting every labelled
person, AVA is 2.90 : 1 on the model's labels and 2.60 : 1 corrected for its
refusals (RESULTS.md, Addenda 2 and 5).

**The dimensions are reported separately and never averaged.** They disagree in
sign here; averaging returns about +1 and would call this corpus neutral, which
is false in both directions at once.

## 6. What is deliberately NOT frozen

The proposal specified a **degree-2 polynomial Ridge equation** over six
features. That is not frozen, and the reason is a measurement result, not a
shortfall:

1. Two of the six features were dropped on evidence: screen time per person
   (no signal) and mean face area (a weaker estimator of the same quantity as
   close-up share). Four dimensions are reported.
2. A degree-2 model has 28 coefficients over the six specified features and 15
   over the four reported dimensions, and the only available labels are
   **synthetic**, produced by our own augmentation operators. Such a model
   learns the operator, not the bias.
3. This failure has already been measured inside the group: C2's learned
   composite scored Spearman **−0.28** against AMI DOME human dominance
   judgements (95% CI −0.51 to −0.08), where their simple parity measure scored
   **+0.465** (CI 0.09 to 0.78). Those are 50 slices from 11 meetings, and their
   own note says a design fixed after seeing the test data is exploratory.

The regression therefore remains in the plan as **the comparison**, to be set
against v1 on the same validation measures. Accuracy on augmented data cannot be
the test, because it measures sensitivity to the operators, not validity.

**Consequence for the TAF.** The novelty claim currently reads "polynomial
Ridge equation". While v1 stands, the defensible claim is a *closed-form,
scale-invariant, externally anchored* equation with a declared applicability
domain. Be exact about what is derived: the presence anchor comes from a
published norm, the tier cut-points (2 and 6) are EuqAud's, and the other three
anchors are declared conventions. The anchoring and the declared domain are the
novelty, not the choice of regressor. The wording needs to change.

## 7. Known portability defect

The close-up threshold is an absolute area cut of 0.08, tuned on film. MELD
faces are smaller: mean relative area 0.027 on its single-face clips against
0.111 on AVA's labelled people (about a quarter), median 0.027 against 0.059
(about a half). The cut finds a close-up in **none of the 371 single-face dev
clips** and in 16 of 3,033 train clips. A portable metric cannot use an absolute
cut; it must be corpus-relative (e.g. a within-corpus quantile). For the MELD
figures the cut is the 75th percentile of dev single-face face area (0.033815),
frozen on dev before train was run. That changes the feature's meaning, from "a
face larger than X" to "the largest-framed quarter of this corpus", and it
cannot say that a whole corpus frames both genders small. Open.

On AVA the 0.08 value is also a convention. Moving it from 0.02 to 0.30 leaves
the sign positive throughout and the tier Moderate up to 0.15, but the score
reads Neutral at 0.20 (+1.78) and 0.30 (+1.08). The other three dimensions do
not use the cut (RESULTS.md, Addendum 9).
